# Video Design

## Goal

Create video that feels deliberately directed and edited rather than assembled from disconnected impressive generated clips.

This reference applies to:

- ads
- reels
- social video
- explainers
- product demos
- brand films
- storyboards
- cinematic sequences
- generated-video workflows

## Start with the sequence's job

Before generating shots, determine:

- audience
- message
- emotional target
- desired action
- duration
- platform
- aspect ratio
- audio strategy
- key information beats
- ending state

Do not begin by generating visually impressive shots without a sequence plan.

## Every shot needs a role

A shot may:

- establish context
- introduce a subject
- show action
- reveal information
- demonstrate scale
- create emotion
- show cause and effect
- transition place or time
- provide evidence
- reset attention
- create contrast

Two shots that perform the same job may not both be necessary.

## Common AI video tells

Watch for accidental:

- constant camera movement
- every shot being cinematic
- excessive slow motion
- endless dolly shots
- floating camera behavior
- arbitrary orbit shots
- fake drone movement
- excessive lens flare
- unnecessary particles
- shallow depth of field in every shot
- speed ramps without editorial purpose
- morphing objects
- changing wardrobe
- changing faces
- inconsistent props
- inconsistent architecture
- unexplained lighting changes
- motion that never settles
- transitions chosen for spectacle rather than continuity
- too many shots that look like isolated image-generation prompts

## Continuity

Across shots, maintain where appropriate:

- character identity
- face
- hair
- wardrobe
- body proportions
- props
- object design
- environment
- geography
- time of day
- lighting direction
- weather
- screen direction
- object placement
- product details
- text and logos

Intentional discontinuity is valid when clearly part of the concept.

## Camera motivation

The camera should not move simply because generated video can move it.

Possible motivated choices:

- locked-off shot for stability
- handheld for immediacy
- pan to reveal information
- tilt to reveal scale
- tracking to follow action
- push-in for emphasis
- pull-back for context
- orbit to explain form
- intentionally chaotic movement for emotion

Use stillness as a legitimate creative choice.

## Editing rhythm

Do not use a fixed shot duration simply because it is easy.

Base pacing on:

- information density
- speech
- action
- music
- emotion
- platform
- audience attention
- visual complexity

Allow moments to breathe.

Fast editing is valid when the concept calls for it.

## Transitions

Default to simple cuts when no special transition is needed.

Use motivated transitions when they improve:

- continuity
- time change
- location change
- comparison
- rhythm
- emotional tone

Avoid transition packs.

## Product demos

For product videos:

- clearly show what changed
- keep UI legible long enough to understand
- avoid fake cursor behavior
- do not move and zoom constantly
- preserve interface state between cuts
- direct attention to one thing at a time

## Audio-aware design

When audio exists, visual pacing should consider:

- dialogue
- narration
- sound effects
- music phrase changes
- silence

Do not cut on every beat automatically.

## Storyboard review

Before generating final clips, verify:

1. Each shot has a clear purpose.
2. The sequence has a beginning, progression, and end.
3. Important subjects remain consistent.
4. Camera movement varies intentionally.
5. There is enough stillness.
6. The viewer can understand what is happening without relying on spectacle.

## Video review question

Ask:

> Does this feel like one directed piece, or a montage of impressive AI clips?

If the latter, improve continuity, shot purpose, pacing, and restraint.
