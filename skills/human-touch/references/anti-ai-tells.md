# Anti-AI Tells

Use this reference as a diagnostic layer.

These are not forbidden visual techniques. Many can be excellent when deliberately requested.
The problem is when they appear automatically, generically, or without contextual justification.

## Common accidental tells

Watch for combinations of:

- unexplained cinematic lighting
- glowing rim lights without a motivated source
- arbitrary volumetric fog
- floating particles added by default
- decorative sparkles
- excessive bloom
- dreamy haze without narrative purpose
- unnecessarily shallow depth of field
- inconsistent pixel grids, blurry sprite edges, and unmotivated pixel noise
- 3D detail that hides weak silhouettes, broken normals, or disconnected parts
- generic hyper-saturated gradients
- glossy plastic treatment across unrelated materials
- meaningless micro-detail
- overly ornate props or clothing
- default centered composition
- unexplained symmetry
- exaggerated reflections
- waxy skin
- structurally impossible details
- decorative futuristic interfaces
- random holograms
- gratuitous glassmorphism
- every area competing for attention
- websites made of repeated rounded card sections
- video sequences made entirely of impressive moving-camera shots

## Intent test

For any suspected AI tell, ask:

1. Was this explicitly requested?
2. Does another active skill or design system call for it?
3. Does it reinforce the concept?
4. Is it structurally and stylistically coherent?
5. Would an art director plausibly choose it deliberately?

If yes, it may be valid.

If no, simplify or remove it.

## Generic beauty

Generated work often converges toward familiar attractive defaults.

For people, avoid automatically producing:

- flawless skin
- perfect symmetry
- tiny noses
- sharp jawlines
- giant eyes
- immaculate eyebrows
- perfectly arranged hair
- generic slight smiles
- identical body types

For environments, avoid automatically producing:

- spotless rooms
- dramatic sunset light
- pristine surfaces
- perfectly arranged objects
- luxury materials
- huge windows
- showroom cleanliness

Keep these when they are actually appropriate to the concept.

## Meaningless detail

Inspect:

- jewelry
- straps
- buckles
- seams
- handles
- hinges
- cables
- furniture
- architecture
- signs
- labels
- background objects
- UI widgets
- website decorations
- video transitions

Ask whether the details are structurally or contextually motivated.

## Trend soup

Avoid stacking unrelated visual trends without a concept.

Possible ingredients include:

- glassmorphism
- neon
- brutalism
- Swiss typography
- Y2K gradients
- grain
- clay icons
- holographic chrome
- editorial serif typography
- cyberpunk lighting

Multiple trends are fine when the creative direction intentionally combines them.

## Final question

Ask:

> Which parts of this visual feel generated because they are generic rather than deliberate?

Fix those first.
